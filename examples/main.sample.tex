\TITLE{Does Social Comparison Shape Blind-Box Design?\\Pricing and Repeat Purchases}
\KEYWORDS{blind boxes; probabilistic selling; social comparison; repeat purchases}

\ABSTRACT{Consumers who stop buying blind boxes may still compare their outcomes
with a peer's success. We study how this persistent social comparison affects a
firm's price and product design in a two-period model. The firm commits to a fixed
price and a target-item probability. Under the maintained full-entry equilibrium,
the model delivers a three-region policy rule: deterministic low-item selling,
a blind box with repeat purchases after either unsuccessful history, and
deterministic high-item selling when comparison pressure exceeds a threshold.}

\section{Introduction}
Firms increasingly sell stochastic outcomes. Video-game loot boxes and hidden-edition
collectibles ask consumers to pay before learning whether they receive a rare item.
These markets make rare outcomes visible: platforms display openings, and brands
promote hidden editions and unboxing content.

\section{Model}
Two consumers observe each other's outcomes over two periods. A purchase yields the
high-value target item $H$ with probability $q$ and a low-value item $L$ otherwise.
The firm commits to a fixed price $p$ and a draw probability $q$.

\section{Fixed Social Comparison}
When the peer already owns the target item, the focal consumer suffers a comparison
loss $\ell$ after drawing $L$, which motivates repeat purchases. A blind box is
optimal when the value gap and comparison pressure are high.

\section{Dynamic Social Comparison}
Conditional on obtaining $L$ in period 1, a consumer faces state $(0,1)$ if her peer
received $H$, or $(0,0)$ if neither did. A fixed price induces one of four response
profiles: UR, CR, NR, or RR.

\section{Managerial Insights}
Stronger social comparison can make the target item more widely available and
eventually eliminate probabilistic selling. The firm does not choose consumers'
comparison sensitivity.

\section{Concluding Remarks}
Persistent exposure can support higher prices without creating additional
consumption value. Broader repeat purchasing gives consumers higher welfare.
